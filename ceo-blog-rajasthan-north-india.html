From the CEO’s travel notes

A journey across Rajasthan and North India moves through very different settings: Jaipur’s architectural identity, a spiritual stop around Gangapur, and Delhi’s dense historic and commercial neighbourhoods. The route deserves careful planning because distances, crowds and local access can shape each day.

1. Jaipur and Hawa Mahal

Hawa Mahal is one of Jaipur’s best-known architectural landmarks. The surrounding old-city streets are part of the experience, with markets, historic facades and busy traffic. Check monument entry arrangements and plan a walking route that accounts for pedestrian congestion.

2. Gangapur and the Dattatreya shrine

The Dattatreya shrine associated with Gangapur is a separate pilgrimage stop in this journey. Confirm the exact location, current access and local arrangements before travel, especially when building a long-distance route or arranging a driver pickup.

3. Delhi and Chandni Chowk

Chandni Chowk is a historic market district with dense lanes, food stops and specialised shopping streets. It is easier to explore on foot or with locally suitable transport than to expect a car to move through every lane. Agree on a meeting point and keep valuables secure.

4. Food, shopping and visitor etiquette

The region offers varied food traditions and markets. Check current prices, hygiene and ingredients before ordering, and compare products before making higher-value purchases. Respect worship-site rules and local crowd-management instructions.

5. A practical multi-city itinerary

Jaipur, Gangapur and Delhi should be treated as a multi-day journey. Confirm the exact shrine and route, reserve accommodation in suitable locations, and allow realistic road and city-transfer buffers. Avoid connecting a long road leg directly to a fixed train or flight without contingency time.

Travel information: Attraction access, opening hours, tickets, local transport, weather and operating arrangements can change.
