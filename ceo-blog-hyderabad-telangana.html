Hyderabad & Telangana: A Journey Through Heritage, Food and Living Culture

By Subodh Kumar Dandapat, CEO — SaahiRides Transit Pvt. Ltd.

A city is best understood not by rushing between landmarks, but by allowing its history, neighbourhoods, food and people to tell their stories.

← All CEO articles

Hyderabad is often introduced through the image of Charminar, the taste of biryani or the scale of its technology corridors. Each is part of the city, but none tells the whole story. Hyderabad brings together the Deccan’s layered history, the energy of a modern working city and the everyday rhythm of markets, lakes and residential neighbourhoods.

Begin with the Old City

The area around Charminar is a natural starting point. The monument, the surrounding lanes and the historic market streets reveal how commerce, faith, craft and food have shaped the city. Nearby, Mecca Masjid and the Chowmahalla Palace offer different windows into Hyderabad’s architectural and cultural past. Visitors should allow time for walking, respect local places of worship and expect busy streets, especially around market hours and festivals.

Understand the city’s history

Golconda Fort deserves time rather than a hurried photo stop. Its gateways, walls and elevated views help explain the strategic importance of the region. The Qutb Shahi Tombs nearby add another dimension to the story of the Deccan. Salar Jung Museum, meanwhile, offers a broad collection for visitors who prefer to explore history and art indoors.

Lakes, gardens and a slower pace

Hussain Sagar and the surrounding public spaces provide a change of pace from the dense historic quarters. Birla Mandir is another well-known stop, with a hilltop setting and views across parts of the city. These places can be combined according to the day’s traffic, weather and the time available; it is better not to over-pack a single itinerary.

Food is part of the journey

Hyderabad’s food culture extends well beyond one famous dish. Visitors may encounter biryani, haleem in its seasonal context, kebabs, Irani chai, Osmania biscuits and a wide range of local vegetarian meals and snacks. Choose established, busy outlets, check ingredients where dietary needs matter and keep drinking water available during warmer months.

Make room for Telangana beyond Hyderabad

Hyderabad can also serve as a base for wider Telangana journeys. Yadadri is an important temple destination; Warangal offers a strong connection to the Kakatiya period; and the region’s reservoirs, forests and rural landscapes show a side of the state that a city-only visit cannot capture. Ramoji Film City is a separate full-day-style outing and should be planned with its own transport and entry arrangements.

A practical way to plan the visit

One day in Hyderabad

Focus on either the Old City and its heritage sites or a carefully selected combination of Golconda and nearby historic attractions. Avoid trying to cover every major landmark in one day.

Two days in Hyderabad

Use the first day for the Old City, palace and museum areas. Keep the second day for Golconda, the Qutb Shahi Tombs, Hussain Sagar and other stops that suit your interests and travel time.

Three days or more

Add a dedicated day trip such as Yadadri or Warangal, or plan Ramoji Film City as its own outing. Confirm current opening schedules, entry requirements and transport arrangements directly before departure.

Travel thoughtfully

Hyderabad traffic can make short distances take longer than expected. Group nearby attractions together, allow buffer time, carry water and keep some flexibility in the plan. For families, senior travellers and visitors unfamiliar with the city, a clear pickup and drop plan can make the day considerably easier.
